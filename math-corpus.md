Fractions: Multiplication, Division, and Word Problems
1. Fraction of a Whole Number
Let's learn to find the fraction of a whole number. Look at the given examples.
 * Example 1: What is \frac{2}{3} of 6?
   * Solution: \frac{2}{3} \text{ of } 6 = \frac{2}{3} \times 6 = \frac{2 \times 6}{3} = \frac{12}{3} = 4.
   * So, \frac{2}{3} \text{ of } 6 = 4.
 * Example 2: What is \frac{3}{8} of 720\text{ g}?
   * Solution: \frac{3}{8} \text{ of } 720\text{ g} = \frac{3}{8} \times 720 = \frac{3 \times 720^{90}}{8_1} = 3 \times 90 = 270\text{ g}.
   * So, \frac{3}{8} \text{ of } 720\text{ g} = 270\text{ g}.
 * Example 3: What is \frac{1}{5} of ₹ 20?
   * Solution: \frac{1}{5} \text{ of } ₹ 20 = \frac{1}{5} \times 20 = \frac{1 \times 20}{5} = \frac{20^4}{5_1} = ₹ 4.
   * So, \frac{1}{5} \text{ of } ₹ 20 = ₹ 4.
2. Multiplication of a Fraction by Another Fraction
Let's learn to multiply fractions. When multiplying a fraction by another fraction, we multiply their numerators to get the numerator of the product fraction, and multiply their denominators to get the denominator of the product fraction.
> If necessary, simplify or reduce the product fraction to its lowest terms. If the product fraction is improper, convert it to a mixed fraction.
> 
Look at the given examples.
(Note: Reducing common factors before finding the result is better than finding it afterwards.)
 * Example 1: Multiply \frac{2}{3} by \frac{3}{5}.
   * Solution: \frac{2}{3} \times \frac{3}{5} = \frac{2 \times 3}{3 \times 5} = \frac{6}{35}.
 * Example 2: Multiply \frac{9}{11} by \frac{7}{18}.
   * Solution: \frac{9}{11} \times \frac{7}{18} = \frac{9^1 \times 7}{11 \times 18_2} = \frac{1 \times 7}{11 \times 2} = \frac{7}{22}.
3. STEM - Multiplication Using an Area Model
Aim: To understand and visualise the concept of multiplying fractions using an area model.
Materials Required:
 * Grid paper / Graph paper
 * Coloured pencils / Markers
 * Rulers
Procedure: Follow the steps given below.
 * Step 1: Draw a large rectangle on the graph paper or grid paper and divide it into equal parts to represent one of the fractions.
   For example, to multiply \frac{3}{5} by \frac{1}{4}, start by dividing the rectangle into 5 equal parts vertically. Shade three parts to represent \frac{3}{5}. Then, divide the same rectangle into four equal parts horizontally. Shade one of these parts with a different colour to represent \frac{1}{4}.
 * Step 2: The overlapping shaded area represents the product of the two fractions.
 * Step 3: Count the total number of equal parts in the entire rectangle (in this case, 20 parts) and the number of overlapping shaded parts (3 parts).
 * Step 4: Write the multiplication equation: \frac{3}{5} \times \frac{1}{4} = \frac{3}{20}.
Practice Problems:
Now, solve the following fraction problems on a grid paper or graph paper using coloured pencils or markers:
 * (a) \frac{1}{3} \times \frac{1}{4}
 * (b) \frac{1}{4} \times \frac{1}{5}
 * (c) \frac{2}{3} \times \frac{1}{4}
4. Multiplication of Mixed Fractions
 * Step 1: When multiplying a mixed fraction by a whole number, convert the mixed fraction to an improper fraction.
 * Step 2: After multiplying, convert the result back to a mixed fraction and simplify the answer, if necessary.
Let's understand using the given examples.
 * Example 1: Multiply 1\frac{5}{8} by 3.
   * Solution:
     1\frac{5}{8} \times 3 = \frac{(1 \times 8) + 5}{8} \times 3 = \frac{8 + 5}{8} \times 3 = \frac{13}{8} \times \frac{3}{1} = \frac{13 \times 3}{8 \times 1} = \frac{39}{8} = 4\frac{7}{8}.
 * Example 2: Multiply 2\frac{4}{7} by 5.
   * Solution:
     2\frac{4}{7} \times 5 = \frac{(2 \times 7) + 4}{7} \times 5 = \frac{14 + 4}{7} \times 5 = \frac{18}{7} \times \frac{5}{1} = \frac{18 \times 5}{7 \times 1} = \frac{90}{7} = 12\frac{6}{7}.
